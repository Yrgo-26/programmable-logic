\section{Reading the bus back}
\label{c17:sec:readback}

A receiving node collects real (destuffed) bits through \code{rx_shift_reg}, gated at the
\code{bit_timer.sample} point (mid-period, once the bus has settled), not at \code{bit_done}. It sets
\code{rx_shift_reg.bit_count} to the same group sizes the transmit side uses, and reads every
completed group out of \code{rx_shift_reg.data}, which is \textbf{right-justified} in its lowest
\code{bit_count} bits (\chapref{c15:ch}). It reassembles the frame in accumulators:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ID}: the high 8 bits from \code{ARB_HI} (\code{rxsr_data(7 downto 0)}), the low 3
    from \code{ARB_LO} (\code{rxsr_data(3 downto 1)}). The group's last bit, \code{rxsr_data(0)}, is
    RTR: dominant marks the data frames this book supports, so a recessive RTR raises \code{error}
    and aborts the frame (a remote frame, unsupported).
  \item \textbf{DLC}: from \code{CTRL} (\code{rxsr_data(3 downto 0)}). The group's top two bits, IDE
    and r0, are \textbf{not} checked: both are always dominant in this book's frames. A receiver
    meeting the recessive IDE of a real extended-identifier frame would misread it as a standard frame
    right up until the CRC check failed; checking IDE and handling what it announces belongs in a
    production controller (\secref{c19:sec:gaps} lists it among the gaps).
  \item \textbf{Data}: every received byte is placed at its \textbf{fixed} position by byte index,
    byte 0 in bits 63 downto 56, byte 1 in 55 downto 48, and so on. This is a position-driven
    \code{case}, \textbf{not} a running left shift (which would only land byte 0 at the top for a
    full 8-byte frame).
\end{itemize}

At the end of \code{STATE_EOF}, latch \code{rx_id}, \code{rx_dlc} and \code{rx_data} from the
accumulators and pulse \code{rx_valid}.

\section{The receive path, state by state}
\label{c17:sec:states}

The same state sequence as \chapref{c16:ch}'s transmit path, walked as a receiver. \code{role = '0'}
throughout. Read this alongside \secref{c16:sec:fields}'s field table: the group sizes are identical,
only what happens to each group differs.

``Pre-empts'' is the \code{rxsr_bit_count}/\code{rxsr_enable} the \textbf{combinational} block drives
while \code{state} still reads the \code{STATE_LOAD_*} value, one cycle before the shift state becomes
visible; \secref{c17:sec:preempt} explains why that is necessary.

\begin{booktable}{@{}p{8.2em}p{4.6em}p{5.6em}L@{}}
  \thead{State} & \thead{Pre-empts} & \thead{Advances on} & \thead{Accumulator / action} \\
  \midrule
  \code{STATE_IDLE} & nothing & dominant \code{rx_bus_s2}, \code{idle_bits} full & latch
    \code{role = '0'}, clear \code{idle_bits} \\
  \code{STATE_START} & nothing & immediately & clear \code{error}, reset the byte index,
    \code{bt_resync} \\
  \code{STATE_SOF} & 1 & \code{rxsr_valid} & collect SOF as a one-bit group; the bit itself is
    discarded \\
  \code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_HI} & 8 & \code{bt_sample} & (waiting: the previous group's last bit is still
    on the wire) \\
  \code{STATE_ARB_HI} & 8 & \code{rxsr_valid} &
    \code{rx_id_acc(10 downto 3) <= rxsr_data(7 downto 0)} \\
  \code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO} & 4 & \code{bt_sample} & \\
  \code{STATE_ARB_LO} & 4 & \code{rxsr_valid} &
    \code{rx_id_acc(2 downto 0) <= rxsr_data(3 downto 1)}; \code{rxsr_data(0)} is RTR: if recessive,
    \code{error <= '1'} and abort \\
  \code{STATE_LOAD_CTRL} & 6 & \code{bt_sample} & \\
  \code{STATE_CTRL} & 6 & \code{rxsr_valid} & \code{rx_dlc_acc <= rxsr_data(3 downto 0)};
    \code{DLC = 0} jumps to \code{STATE_CRC_WAIT} \\
  \code{STATE_LOAD_DATA} & 8 & \code{bt_sample} & \\
  \code{STATE_DATA} & 8 & \code{rxsr_valid} & place \code{rxsr_data} at the fixed position for byte
    \code{data_byte_idx} \\
  \code{STATE_CRC_WAIT} & nothing & immediately & one settling cycle; \textbf{no} wait for
    \code{bt_sample} \\
  \code{STATE_LOAD_CRC_HI} & 8 & \code{bt_sample} & \\
  \code{STATE_CRC_HI} & 8 & \code{rxsr_valid} & keep feeding \code{crc15}; the value itself is not
    kept \\
  \code{STATE_LOAD_CRC_LO} & 7 & \code{bt_sample} & \\
  \code{STATE_CRC_LO} & 7 & \code{rxsr_valid} & the last bits into \code{crc15} \\
  \code{STATE_CRC_LO_WAIT} & nothing & \code{bt_bit_done} & check \code{crc_valid}: if low,
    \code{error <= '1'} and abort \\
  \code{STATE_CRC_DELIM} & nothing & \code{bt_bit_done} & \\
  \code{STATE_ACK_SLOT} & nothing & \code{bt_bit_done} & drive the bus dominant to acknowledge \\
  \code{STATE_ACK_DELIM} & nothing & \code{bt_bit_done} & arm the 7-bit EOF countdown \\
  \code{STATE_EOF} & nothing & \code{bt_bit_done} ×7 & at the last bit, latch \code{rx_id},
    \code{rx_dlc}, \code{rx_data}, pulse \code{rx_valid} \\
\end{booktable}

Two patterns run through the whole table. Every \code{STATE_LOAD_*} row exists purely to consume the
previous group's closing bit period: the state machine harvests nothing there, even though the
\code{bt_sample} ending the state is exactly the one \code{rx_shift_reg} collects the next group's
first bit on. Every closing (unstuffed) row advances on \code{bt_bit_done} instead of
\code{bt_sample}, since those fields bypass \code{rx_shift_reg} entirely and are counted as whole bit
periods.

\subsection*{Why \code{STATE_SOF} enables \code{rx_shift_reg} for a bit nobody keeps}
The collected value is discarded; SOF is always dominant and \code{STATE_IDLE} already knew that when
it started this frame. What matters is that the bit passes through the destuffing's run tracker. That
tracker (\code{consecutive}, \code{last_bit}) survives group boundaries and is cleared only by reset
(\chapref{c15:ch}), and the transmitter's copy inside \code{tx_shift_reg} counted SOF, since
\chapref{c16:ch} sends it through \code{tx_shift_reg} like any other stuffed field. Skip it here, and
the receiver runs the whole frame one dominant bit behind the transmitter.

That is no small window. Every identifier whose top four bits are zero, that is, \textbf{every ID
below \code{0x080}}, gives SOF plus four dominant identifier bits: the transmitter's run reaches five
and it inserts a stuff bit, while a receiver that missed SOF stands at only four, expects none, and
takes it for a real bit. Everything after it is shifted. The failure turns up far later as a CRC error
or a stuffing violation, and it strikes precisely the high-priority identifiers a real design reserves
for its most important messages.

The rule underneath is worth stating on its own: \textbf{both run trackers have to see exactly the
same bits, SOF through the end of the CRC field, and nothing else.} The tail is excluded on both sides
since neither shift register is enabled for it. Across \emph{frame boundaries}, on the other hand,
``the same bits'' is no guarantee the design can lean on at all, which is
\secref{c17:sec:cleanslate}.

\code{STATE_SOF} needs no pre-emption for this. \code{STATE_START} pulses \code{bt_resync}, so the
SOF bit's sample point is a whole \code{SAMPLE_TICK} away (\chapref{c12:ch}); the state machine is
well inside \code{STATE_SOF} by then, and an ordinary combinational enable is enough.

\section{A clean slate between frames}
\label{c17:sec:cleanslate}

The run trackers are cleared only by their reset (\chapref{c14:ch}, \ref{c15:ch}), and within a
single frame that persistence is exactly right. Let it stretch \emph{across} frame boundaries, and a
subtle asymmetry appears: a tracker's state at SOF now depends on which bits that particular node
happened to push through it earlier, and different nodes have different histories. A node that
transmitted the previous frame ends it with its \code{tx_shift_reg} tracker holding the CRC field's
tail; a node that only received holds that history in its \code{rx_shift_reg} tracker instead, and its
transmit tracker still shows whatever its \emph{own} most recent transmission left, or nothing at all
after reset.

Now let two such nodes start transmitting at the same SOF, which is exactly what arbitration exists
for. Identical identifier bits come out of both, but the node carrying a leftover run reaches five
identical bits \emph{earlier} and inserts its stuff bit one position before the other node. At that
position it sends a recessive stuff bit while the other node is still sending a real dominant bit; the
wired-AND reads dominant, the stuffing node is sending recessive inside the arbitration field, and it
concludes that it has lost arbitration. No contest took place: the \emph{would-be winner} walks away
from its own frame with \code{error} raised. This is not hypothetical; it is exactly how
\code{can_controller_tb}'s case 2 fails if the trackers persist, with node A entering it still holding
the tail of case 1's CRC.

Real CAN has no such failure, since the stuff bit count starts afresh at every SOF, and this design
gets the same guarantee with a single signal: both shift registers are \textbf{held in reset while
the bus is idle}, so every frame starts with clean trackers on every node, whatever each of them did
before. In the declarative part:

\begin{vhdlcode}
    -- Shift-register reset: asserted while the bus is idle, so every frame
    -- starts with clean run trackers on every node.
    signal srs_reset_n : std_logic;
\end{vhdlcode}

after \code{begin}:

\begin{vhdlcode}
    srs_reset_n <= '0' when ((reset_s2_n = '0') or (state = STATE_IDLE)) else '1';
\end{vhdlcode}

and both shift register instances change their second association from \code{reset_s2_n} to
\code{srs_reset_n}, so that the port maps from \chapref{c14:ch} and \ref{c15:ch} become:

\begin{vhdlcode}
    tx_shift_reg1: entity work.tx_shift_reg
        port map(clock, srs_reset_n, txsr_load, txsr_data, txsr_bit_count, txsr_shift,
                 txsr_tx_bit, txsr_stuff, txsr_done, txsr_bit_valid);

    rx_shift_reg1: entity work.rx_shift_reg
        port map(clock, srs_reset_n, bt_sample, rx_bus_s2, rxsr_bit_count, rxsr_enable,
                 rxsr_data, rxsr_valid, rxsr_stuff_error, rxsr_real_bit, rxsr_real_bit_valid);
\end{vhdlcode}

The reset releases at the edge leaving \code{STATE_IDLE}, a whole cycle before the SOF load is clocked
into \code{tx_shift_reg}, so the registers are awake in time for every frame. Note what this does
\emph{not} change: within a frame nothing is ever cleared, so the chained reload behaviour
\chapref{c14:ch}'s test 3 pins down (a run continuing across a group boundary) is untouched, and the
SOF-skip argument above holds bit for bit. The clean slate begins at \code{STATE_IDLE}, and
\code{STATE_IDLE} is reachable only between frames.

\section{Feeding \code{crc15} on the receive side}
\label{c17:sec:crc}

The mirror image of \chapref{c16:ch}'s transmit-side gating: enable \code{crc15} on
\code{rx_shift_reg.real_bit_valid} (which already excludes both ``no new bit yet'' and every discarded
stuff bit), fed with \code{rx_shift_reg.real_bit}, over SOF through the CRC field. Feeding the
received CRC field's own bits back returns the register to exactly zero if the frame arrived intact
(\chapref{c13:ch}'s generate-then-check), which \code{crc_valid} reports.

There is one \code{crc15} and one enable, so this replaces \chapref{c16:ch}'s assignment rather than
standing beside it. \code{role} now chooses the source instead of switching the engine off:

\begin{vhdlcode}
crc_enable  <= (txsr_bit_valid and not txsr_stuff and role) or
               (rxsr_real_bit_valid and not role);
crc_data_in <= txsr_tx_bit when role = '1' else rxsr_real_bit;
\end{vhdlcode}

Leave \chapref{c16:ch}'s version in place and a receiving node never accumulates anything, so
\code{crc_valid} reads \code{'1'} (the reset value) throughout and the check in
\code{STATE_CRC_LO_WAIT} passes for every frame, corrupted or not.

\code{crc_clear} is untouched by any of this and stands exactly as \chapref{c16:ch} wrote it, a pulse
in \code{STATE_START} whatever the role. It matters more now than it did then: this is the chapter
where the node gets three ways to leave a frame early, and each leaves the engine in the middle of a
message it will never finish. None of the abort paths below has to clear it on the way out, since the
next frame clears it on the way in.

\section{Waiting for one extra sample: the decisive timing lesson}
\label{c17:sec:preempt}

On the transmit side, \code{tx_shift_reg.done} already spends an extra bit period letting a group's
last bit run out before the next group is loaded. A receiver reacts to \code{rx_shift_reg.valid} with
no such built-in wait, so \code{can_controller} has to add it explicitly at each of the RX side's
\code{STATE_LOAD_*} states: \textbf{wait for one more \code{bt_sample}} before moving on, since the
wire still shows the previous group's last bit for the whole of its closing period.

There is a second catch: \code{state} becomes the shift state only \textbf{one cycle after} the
\code{bt_sample} that triggered the transition, one cycle too late to enable \code{rx_shift_reg} for
that very sample. That is why the combinational control block \textbf{pre-empts} \code{rxsr_enable}
(and sets the coming group's \code{bit_count}) for exactly that sample while it is still in the
\code{STATE_LOAD_*} state.

\paragraph{What it looks like cycle by cycle}
Here is a group boundary on the receive side, where the 8-bit-wide high ID group hands over to the
4-bit-wide low ID plus RTR group. The cycles are the 50 MHz clock's, so the two \code{bt_sample}
pulses are 50 cycles apart; the run of identical cycles between them is elided.

\begin{textdrawing}
clock cycle    bt_sample             state  rxsr_bit_count  rxsr_enable  rxsr_valid
n                      0         STATE_ARB_HI               8            1           0
n + 1                  1         STATE_ARB_HI               8            1           0
n + 2                  0         STATE_ARB_HI               8            1           1
n + 3                  0    STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO               4            1           0
...                    0    STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO               4            1           0
n + 51                 1    STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO               4            1           0
n + 52                 0         STATE_ARB_LO               4            1           0
\end{textdrawing}

Read the two sample cycles against each other. At \textbf{n + 1} the eighth bit of the high ID group
is taken, and \code{rxsr_valid} appears one cycle later at \textbf{n + 2}, which is when the state
machine decides to move on. \code{state} does not read \code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO} until \textbf{n + 3}.

Now look at \textbf{n + 51}, the sample that has to catch the \emph{first} bit of the low ID group.
\code{state} still reads \code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO} at that moment. It does not become
\code{STATE_ARB_LO} until \textbf{n + 52}, one cycle after the bit has already come and gone. That is
the whole problem in one line: the state that names the group always arrives one cycle later than the
sample that starts it.

The pre-emption is what makes \code{rxsr_bit_count = 4} and \code{rxsr_enable = '1'} true at
\textbf{n + 51} all the same, by driving them from the \code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO} arm instead of the
\code{STATE_ARB_LO} arm. Drive them from \code{STATE_ARB_LO} instead, and the same boundary looks like
this:

\begin{textdrawing}
clock cycle    bt_sample             state  rxsr_bit_count  rxsr_enable  rxsr_valid
n + 3                  0    STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO               0            0           0
...                    0    STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO               0            0           0
n + 51                 1    STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO               0            0           0
n + 52                 0         STATE_ARB_LO               4            1           0
\end{textdrawing}

\code{rx_shift_reg} is disabled across the one sample that mattered. The first bit of the
identifier's low group is never collected, so the group completes one bit short against the wire,
every later group is shifted by one, and \code{crc15} is fed a stream that never matches. Nothing
reports any error at \textbf{n + 51}; the failure turns up at \code{STATE_CRC_LO_WAIT}, some forty bit
periods later, as a CRC that does not go to zero.

Note that this costs nothing extra on the wire. \code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO} already consumes the previous
group's closing bit period, which is why it advances on \code{bt_sample} and collects nothing of its
own; the pre-emption merely puts the register in the right configuration before that period is over.

\begin{warning}
This pattern repeats at every group boundary and is the design's single most important lesson: \textbf{a
signal that looks ``done'' one cycle too early or too late does not cancel itself out across group
boundaries; it accumulates into a silent misalignment} between transmitter and receiver that only much
later turns up as a CRC error or a stuffing violation, far from its cause.
\end{warning}

\section{Detecting lost arbitration}
\label{c17:sec:arbitration}

Exactly \secref{c10:sec:arbitration}'s rule \textemdash{} \canref{4} is the fuller version
\textemdash{} checked once per bit at the sample point, during \code{STATE_ARB_HI} and
\code{STATE_ARB_LO} alone, which is precisely the arbitration field, ID plus RTR. Unique IDs guarantee
that arbitration is settled within the 11-bit identifier; the check harmlessly covers RTR as well,
where every node here sends dominant, a bit that can never lose:

\begin{vhdlcode}
if ((bt_sample = '1') and (txsr_tx_bit = '1') and (rx_bus_s2 = '0')) then
    error     <= '1';    -- Sent recessive, read dominant: lost.
    tx_done   <= '1';    -- The attempt is over; say so, win or lose.
    role      <= '0';
    idle_bits <= 0;
    state     <= STATE_IDLE;
end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

The check lives inside \code{STATE_ARB_HI}'s and \code{STATE_ARB_LO}'s \code{if (role = '1')} branch,
and that is what bounds it to a transmitting node; the three terms above are the sensing condition
itself.

\paragraph{Why \code{tx_done} pulses here too, on a frame that was never sent}
It reads wrongly at first: \code{tx_done} means ``the frame completed'', and this one did not. Read it
instead as \emph{``the transmission attempt is over, and the port is yours again''} \textemdash{}
which is the only question a caller can act on. \code{error} is what says whether the attempt
succeeded, and together the two carry the whole outcome: \code{tx_done} alone means sent,
\code{tx_done} with \code{error} means lost.

Leave it out, and the caller has no edge at all to wait for. A node losing arbitration returns to
\code{STATE_IDLE} and is perfectly able to transmit again, but nothing ever announces that, so any
caller waiting for \code{tx_done} before asking again waits forever. That is not hypothetical: it is
exactly how \code{register_bank} (\chapref{c18:ch}) sets its TX-ready bit, and without this pulse a
single lost arbitration \textemdash{} the normal case on a two-node bus \textemdash{} would leave the
whole node unable to transmit until it was reset.

Note that it is \code{rx_bus_s2}, the synchronised copy, and not the \code{rx_bus} port.
\Secref{c11:sec:sync}'s rule applies everywhere, this comparison included: nothing in the design reads
the raw pin. It would be easy to talk your way out of this one, since the sample point is 35 ticks
into a bit that settled near tick 0, so the level is certainly stable by then. That argument is about
\emph{when} the value is stable on the wire, and metastability is about what a flip-flop does when it
samples a signal no clock in this chip controls. The two extra flip-flops cost two clock ticks out of
fifty and buy the one comparison the whole arbitration scheme rests on.

Clearing \code{idle_bits} is not bookkeeping tidiness. Without it the node lands back in
\code{STATE_IDLE} with \code{idle_bits} full while the winner is in the middle of its frame, so the
winner's next dominant bit is read as a new SOF, the node enters \code{STATE_START}, and
\code{STATE_START} wipes the \code{error} it has just raised. The same clear is required at every other
abort below, for the same reason.

\section{Completing the processes}
\label{c17:sec:complete}

The same rules as in \chapref{c16:ch}'s walkthrough: the code fragments are successive slices in the
order the code reads, and nothing from \chapref{c16:ch} is rewritten beyond its marked placeholders;
every slice below fills one of them in.

\subsection*{The combinational process}
It gets two new blocks, and \code{crc_valid} joins its sensitivity list (the ACK driving below reads
it). First, between the transmit side's \code{case} for the shift gating and the \code{case} statement
for the bus driving, the receive side's enable and group widths, which is the pre-emption made
concrete:

\begin{vhdlcode}
        -- The receive-side enable and chunk widths: the pre-empt. Active
        -- through every stuffed field's LOAD and shifting state, so the sample
        -- that falls while state still reads STATE_LOAD_* is collected at the
        -- new width.
        if (role = '0') then
            case state is
                when STATE_SOF =>
                    rxsr_enable    <= '1';
                    rxsr_bit_count <= "0001";
                when STATE_LOAD_ARB_HI | STATE_ARB_HI
                   | STATE_LOAD_DATA   | STATE_DATA
                   | STATE_LOAD_CRC_HI | STATE_CRC_HI =>
                    rxsr_enable    <= '1';
                    rxsr_bit_count <= "1000";
                when STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO | STATE_ARB_LO =>
                    rxsr_enable    <= '1';
                    rxsr_bit_count <= "0100";
                when STATE_LOAD_CTRL | STATE_CTRL =>
                    rxsr_enable    <= '1';
                    rxsr_bit_count <= "0110";
                when STATE_LOAD_CRC_LO | STATE_CRC_LO =>
                    rxsr_enable    <= '1';
                    rxsr_bit_count <= "0111";
                when others =>
                    null;
            end case;
        end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

Every \code{STATE_LOAD_*} state pre-empts the \emph{coming} group's width, one \code{bt_sample} before
\code{state} visibly changes, exactly as \secref{c17:sec:preempt} derived; grouping it with its shift
state in one and the same arm is what makes that automatic. Second, the receiver's single dominant
bit: a new arm in the \code{case} statement for the bus driving, above its \code{when others}:

\begin{vhdlcode}
            when STATE_ACK_SLOT =>
                if ((role = '0') and (crc_valid = '1')) then
                    bus_en <= '1';
                    tx_bus <= '0';
                end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

\subsection*{The sequential process}
Every \code{-- role = '0': L17.} marker becomes the arm's second half. There are only two receive
shapes. A receiving \code{STATE_LOAD_*} state waits for one extra \code{bt_sample} (the same
\code{elsif} in all six), and a receiving shift state advances on \code{rxsr_valid} and harvests its
group if the frame keeps any of it. Here are both shapes filled in, on \code{STATE_SOF} and the
identifier's high pair; note that \code{STATE_ARB_HI}'s transmit half grew too, taking up
\secref{c17:sec:arbitration}'s arbitration check before the \code{done} test. (The two can never fire
in the same cycle, since \code{bt_sample} is mid-period and \code{done} follows a \code{bit_done}, so
the order is about readability rather than priority; a loss on a group's last bit still simply wins by
coming first, at that bit's sample point.)

\begin{vhdlcode}
                when STATE_SOF =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        if (txsr_done = '1') then
                            state <= STATE_LOAD_ARB_HI;
                        end if;
                    else
                        if (rxsr_valid = '1') then
                            state <= STATE_LOAD_ARB_HI;
                        end if;
                    end if;
                when STATE_LOAD_ARB_HI =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        state <= STATE_ARB_HI;
                    elsif (bt_sample = '1') then
                        state <= STATE_ARB_HI;
                    end if;
                when STATE_ARB_HI =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        if ((bt_sample = '1') and (txsr_tx_bit = '1') and (rx_bus_s2 = '0')) then
                            error     <= '1';    -- Sent recessive, read dominant: lost.
                            tx_done   <= '1';
                            role      <= '0';
                            idle_bits <= 0;
                            state     <= STATE_IDLE;
                        elsif (txsr_done = '1') then
                            state <= STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO;
                        end if;
                    else
                        if (rxsr_valid = '1') then
                            rx_id_acc(10 downto 3) <= rxsr_data(7 downto 0);
                            state <= STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO;
                        end if;
                    end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

\code{STATE_LOAD_ARB_LO}, \code{STATE_LOAD_CTRL}, \code{STATE_LOAD_DATA}, \code{STATE_LOAD_CRC_HI} and
\code{STATE_LOAD_CRC_LO} all take the identical \code{elsif (bt_sample = '1')} wait, and
\code{STATE_CRC_HI}/\code{STATE_CRC_LO} take the identical \code{rxsr_valid} advance without harvesting
anything: their bits reach \code{crc15} bit by bit through \code{real_bit}, and the assembled groups
mean nothing. The states with real receive work in them:

\begin{vhdlcode}
                when STATE_ARB_LO =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        if ((bt_sample = '1') and (txsr_tx_bit = '1') and (rx_bus_s2 = '0')) then
                            error     <= '1';
                            tx_done   <= '1';
                            role      <= '0';
                            idle_bits <= 0;
                            state     <= STATE_IDLE;
                        elsif (txsr_done = '1') then
                            state <= STATE_LOAD_CTRL;
                        end if;
                    else
                        if (rxsr_valid = '1') then
                            rx_id_acc(2 downto 0) <= rxsr_data(3 downto 1);
                            if (rxsr_data(0) = '1') then
                                error     <= '1';    -- Recessive RTR: a remote frame.
                                idle_bits <= 0;
                                state     <= STATE_IDLE;
                            else
                                state <= STATE_LOAD_CTRL;
                            end if;
                        end if;
                    end if;
                when STATE_CTRL =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        if (txsr_done = '1') then
                            if (unsigned(tx_dlc) = 0) then
                                state <= STATE_CRC_WAIT;
                            else
                                state <= STATE_LOAD_DATA;
                            end if;
                        end if;
                    else
                        if (rxsr_valid = '1') then
                            rx_dlc_acc <= rxsr_data(3 downto 0);
                            if (unsigned(rxsr_data(3 downto 0)) = 0) then
                                state <= STATE_CRC_WAIT;
                            else
                                state <= STATE_LOAD_DATA;
                            end if;
                        end if;
                    end if;
                when STATE_DATA =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        if (txsr_done = '1') then
                            if (data_byte_idx + 1 = to_integer(unsigned(tx_dlc))) then
                                state <= STATE_CRC_WAIT;
                            else
                                data_byte_idx <= data_byte_idx + 1;
                                state         <= STATE_LOAD_DATA;
                            end if;
                        end if;
                    else
                        if (rxsr_valid = '1') then
                            case data_byte_idx is    -- Fixed position per byte index.
                                when 0 => rx_data_acc(63 downto 56) <= rxsr_data;
                                when 1 => rx_data_acc(55 downto 48) <= rxsr_data;
                                when 2 => rx_data_acc(47 downto 40) <= rxsr_data;
                                when 3 => rx_data_acc(39 downto 32) <= rxsr_data;
                                when 4 => rx_data_acc(31 downto 24) <= rxsr_data;
                                when 5 => rx_data_acc(23 downto 16) <= rxsr_data;
                                when 6 => rx_data_acc(15 downto  8) <= rxsr_data;
                                when others => rx_data_acc(7 downto 0) <= rxsr_data;
                            end case;
                            if (data_byte_idx + 1 = to_integer(unsigned(rx_dlc_acc))) then
                                state <= STATE_CRC_WAIT;
                            else
                                data_byte_idx <= data_byte_idx + 1;
                                state         <= STATE_LOAD_DATA;
                            end if;
                        end if;
                    end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

Two pre-edge reads to notice, both the same rule as \chapref{c14:ch}'s \code{MAX_RUN - 1}.
\code{STATE_CTRL} decides the DLC branch directly from \code{rxsr_data(3 downto 0)}, since
\code{rx_dlc_acc} is being assigned at this very edge and still reads the previous frame's value. And
\code{STATE_DATA} compares against \code{rx_dlc_acc}, which by then \emph{is} stable, latched a whole
field earlier.

The tail differs from \chapref{c16:ch}'s in exactly two arms. \code{STATE_CRC_LO_WAIT} is where the
receiver consumes the last CRC bit's closing period and passes its verdict, and \code{STATE_EOF}'s
last bit is where a receiver publishes the frame:

\begin{vhdlcode}
                when STATE_CRC_LO_WAIT =>
                    if (role = '1') then
                        state <= STATE_CRC_DELIM;    -- One clock cycle, not a bit period.
                    elsif (bt_bit_done = '1') then
                        if (crc_valid = '1') then
                            state <= STATE_CRC_DELIM;
                        else
                            error     <= '1';        -- The engine did not return to zero.
                            idle_bits <= 0;
                            state     <= STATE_IDLE;
                        end if;
                    end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

\begin{vhdlcode}
                when STATE_EOF =>
                    if (bt_bit_done = '1') then
                        if (field_bit_count = 0) then
                            if (role = '1') then
                                tx_done <= '1';
                            else
                                rx_id    <= rx_id_acc;
                                rx_dlc   <= rx_dlc_acc;
                                rx_data  <= rx_data_acc;
                                rx_valid <= '1';
                            end if;
                            state <= STATE_IDLE;
                        else
                            field_bit_count <= field_bit_count - 1;
                        end if;
                    end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

Finally an overlay that belongs to no single state. After the whole \code{case} statement, still
inside the clocked branch:

\begin{vhdlcode}
            -- A stuffing violation aborts a reception from any stuffed state.
            if ((role = '0') and (rxsr_stuff_error = '1')) then
                error     <= '1';
                idle_bits <= 0;
                state     <= STATE_IDLE;
            end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

Placed after the \code{case} statement it wins over whatever the state arm decided at the same edge,
which is the whole point: a violation is fatal wherever it fires, and a sample can fire in any of the
stuffed fields' arms, the LOAD states included (their pre-empted sample is treated like any other).
Writing it once is better than repeating three lines in a dozen arms.

\subsection*{Acknowledging, checking and aborting}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The ACK slot:} a receiver whose \code{crc_valid} is set drives the bus dominant for
    that single bit to acknowledge the frame.
  \item \textbf{The CRC check:} in \code{STATE_CRC_LO_WAIT} a receiver reads \code{crc_valid}. If it
    is set it waits for \code{bt_bit_done} and then moves on to \code{STATE_CRC_DELIM}; if it is not,
    it aborts.
  \item \textbf{Stuffing violation:} \code{rx_shift_reg.stuff_error} aborts a reception from any
    state. A transmitter cannot violate the bit stuffing rule, so this applies to RX only.
  \item \textbf{Arbitration loss} is the only abort that happens while \emph{transmitting}, and
    therefore the only one that also pulses \code{tx_done}. The receive-side aborts end a reception,
    and a reception has no caller waiting for a completion pulse: \code{rx_valid} simply fails to
    appear, which is exactly right, since no frame arrived.
  \item \textbf{\code{error} holds:} cleared in \code{STATE_START}, held otherwise, so that a caller
    sees it right up until the next frame begins. That is precisely why \code{STATE_IDLE} requires an
    \textbf{idle bus} before the node leaves that state at all, and why every abort here has to clear
    \code{idle_bits} on the way out. Every abort lands back in \code{STATE_IDLE} in the middle of a
    frame, while the bus is still busy. Without the idle-bus requirement the node would enter
    \code{STATE_START} again within a few bit periods and wipe the \code{error} it raised, so that a
    caller polling \code{error} would never see it. Requiring eleven recessive bit periods first is
    exactly the guarantee ``right up until the next frame begins'' issues.
\end{itemize}

\section{Verifying it: \code{can_controller_tb}}
\label{c17:sec:verify}

Now that the receive path is complete, \file{controller/can_controller_tb.vhd} finally runs all the
way. It connects \textbf{two} \code{can_controller} nodes to a single open-drain bus, combining every
node's pair \code{(tx_bus, bus_en)} the way \secref{c10:sec:can} described a wired-AND, and runs two
cases.

\paragraph{Case 1, reception}
Node A sends ID \code{0x123}, DLC 5, data \code{11 22 F8 44 55} while node B receives. It checks
\code{tx_done} and \code{rx_valid}, and that B recovers the ID, the DLC and \textbf{every} data byte
at its fixed position. The byte \code{0xF8} is chosen to force a stuff bit inside the data field, so
this exercises the transmit path, the receive path, the destuffing and the multi-byte placement in one
and the same frame. A single node cannot test any of it: \code{rx_shift_reg} is active only while
\code{role = '0'}, so a transmitter never receives its own frame.

\paragraph{Case 2, arbitration}
Both nodes request during the same cycle, A with ID \code{0x000} and B with \code{0x001}, separated
only in the last identifier bit. B has to detect the loss at exactly that bit and stop driving the
bus; A has to complete as usual, unaware that a contest took place. The last assertion is the one to
notice: it reads B's \code{error} \emph{after} A's frame has completed, so it fails unless both the
lingering \code{error} and the \code{idle_bits} discipline above hold.
