\chapter{The register bank and SPI from the waveform}
\label{c18:ch}

{\large\itshape This is where the half begins that makes the controller reachable from a
microcontroller.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Why \code{tx_done} and \code{rx_valid} as one-cycle pulses are unusable for a polling driver,
    and what the register map promises instead: sticky, pollable levels with explicit clears.
  \item \code{register_bank}: the STATUS latching, \code{TX_SEND} as a write-triggered event,
    capturing a received frame, and masking on write.
  \item SPI from the waveform upwards: modes, MSB first, SS as framing.
  \item Why the slave does \textbf{not} clock on SCK but oversamples it in the 50 MHz domain.
  \item The \code{spi_slave} handed out: the byte engine getting \code{SCK}, \code{MOSI} and
    \code{SS} safely into the system clock domain and handing over one clean byte at a time.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

\code{can_controller} is finished as of \chapref{c17:ch}. This chapter builds the first of the two
layers that make it reachable from outside. The module can be written long before the controller is
finished \textemdash{} it needs only \code{can_def} \textemdash{} so it is a full work stream that can
start in parallel.

The authoritative source for every register number in this chapter is appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.
Where the chapter and the specification disagree, the specification wins.
