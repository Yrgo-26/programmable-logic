\chapter{Receive shift register and destuffing}
\label{c15:ch}

{\large\itshape The receive side's mirror image: deserialisation, removal of stuff bits, and flagging
of stuffing errors.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{rx_shift_reg}: deserialisation, destuffing and detection of stuffing errors.
  \item Sampling mid bit period (\code{bit_timer.sample}) rather than at \code{bit_done}.
  \item \code{real_bit} and \code{real_bit_valid}: feeding \code{crc15} one destuffed bit at a time.
  \item Instantiating \code{rx_shift_reg} inside \code{can_controller}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

\code{rx_shift_reg} is the receive side's mirror image of \chapref{c14:ch}'s \code{tx_shift_reg}: it
deserialises a group MSB first from the bus and \textbf{discards} the stuff bits \code{tx_shift_reg}
inserted, and flags a violation if an expected stuff bit fails to appear. The run counting and the
rule about five identical bits in a row are identical, only reversed \textemdash{} \canref{3} is the
same rule seen from the protocol, and it is worth reading once more from here, since it is the one
rule in the book that two modules have to agree on independently. \code{can_controller}
(\chapref{c17:ch}) reuses \textbf{one single} \code{rx_shift_reg} for every stuffed field of an
incoming frame, changing its \code{bit_count} between groups; the unstuffed tail (CRC delimiter, ACK,
ACK delimiter, EOF) it reads directly, past this register.
