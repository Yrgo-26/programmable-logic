\section{Interface}
\label{c15:sec:iface}

\modulefig[0.62]{lectures/L15/appendix/images/rx_shift_reg.png}

\begin{booktable}{@{}p{6.8em}p{3.4em}p{10em}L@{}}
  \thead{Port} & \thead{Dir.} & \thead{Type} & \thead{Meaning} \\
  \midrule
  \code{clock}, \code{reset_s2_n} & in & \code{std_logic} & 50 MHz clock, synchronised active-low
    reset. \\
  \code{sample} & in & \code{std_logic} & One pulse per CAN bit (\code{bit_timer.sample}) marking
    when \code{rx_bus} is to be read. \\
  \code{rx_bus} & in & \code{std_logic} & The bus level, valid at the sample point. \\
  \code{bit_count} & in & \code{std_logic_vector(3 downto 0)} & Number of \textbf{real} bits to
    collect, 1--8; read live, may change between groups. \\
  \code{enable} & in & \code{std_logic} & Decides whether \code{sample} pulses are acted on. \\
  \code{data} & out & \code{byte_t} & The collected group, \textbf{right-justified} in its lowest
    \code{bit_count} bits. \\
  \code{valid} & out & \code{std_logic} & Pulses when \code{bit_count} real bits have been
    collected. \\
  \code{stuff_error} & out & \code{std_logic} & Pulses if an expected stuff bit does not invert. \\
  \code{real_bit} & out & \code{std_logic} & The single destuffed bit accepted at this sample. \\
  \code{real_bit_valid} & out & \code{std_logic} & \code{'1'} at a sample that accepted a real bit;
    \code{'0'} at a discarded stuff bit. \\
\end{booktable}

\code{data} and \code{real_bit} are levels; \code{valid}, \code{stuff_error} and
\code{real_bit_valid} are \textbf{one-cycle pulses}, defaulted to \code{'0'} at the top of the
clocked process, exactly like \code{tx_shift_reg}'s. \code{real_bit_valid} is the one that bites:
\code{can_controller} gates \code{crc15}'s enable directly on it (\chapref{c17:ch}), so a level
lingering until the next sample feeds the same bit in for a whole bit period and silently destroys
the CRC. The testbench checks that it has fallen again the cycle after every sample, for exactly that
reason.

There is no \code{load}: the sampling happens mid-period (\code{sample}, not \code{bit_done}), once
every node's signals have had time to settle. A transmitter changes the bus at a period boundary, so
a receiver also acting on \code{bit_done} would read at the very moment the level changes;
\secref{c12:sub:bttrace}'s tick trace shows the two pulses and the 14 ticks between them.
\code{can_controller} only changes \code{bit_count} between groups; \code{valid} itself reports every
group's completion. \code{rx_shift_reg_tb} binds \textbf{positionally}, so the order and the types
above are what has to match; the names are yours.

\section{What the module has to remember}
\label{c15:sec:state}

As in \chapref{c14:ch}: list the state before any process is written. The receive side has the same
two jobs, mirrored \textemdash{} keeping a group \emph{filling up}, and keeping the bit stuffing rule
honest \textemdash{} plus one it did not have: \emph{predicting} where the transmitter must have
inserted a stuff bit. Every rule below speaks in terms of exactly these five signals:

\begin{vhdlcode}
signal data_reg      : byte_t;                     -- Collected real bits, filling from the right.
signal collected     : natural range 0 to 8;       -- Real bits accepted toward this chunk so far.
signal last_bit      : std_logic;                  -- The bit most recently sampled off the bus.
signal consecutive   : natural range 0 to MAX_RUN; -- Current run length; 0 = none.
signal stuff_expected: std_logic;                  -- The next sampled bit must be a stuff bit.
\end{vhdlcode}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\code{data_reg} and \code{collected} are the group filling up.} Every accepted bit is
    shifted in from the right (\code{data_reg(6 downto 0) & rx_bus}), which is where the
    right-justification comes from, and \code{collected} counts accepted bits towards
    \code{bit_count}. The register behind \code{data} has to be this internal signal, with
    \code{data <= data_reg;} as a concurrent assignment outside the process: an \code{out} port
    cannot be \emph{read}, and a shift is a read-modify-write.
  \item \textbf{\code{last_bit} and \code{consecutive} are the same run tracker as \chapref{c14:ch}'s},
    character for character: the bit most recently on the wire, and how long the identical run it
    ended is. Both sides of the link run the same tracker over the same bits, against the same
    \code{MAX_RUN} from \code{can_def}; that is what keeps them agreeing on where the stuff bits
    belong. As in \chapref{c14:ch}, the pair is written from a single procedure the process declares.
  \item \textbf{\code{stuff_expected} is the mirror image of \chapref{c14:ch}'s
    \code{stuff_pending}} \textemdash{} the same flag with the opposite job. The transmitter's flag
    says ``I \emph{owe} a stuff bit next''; the receiver's says ``the next bit had better \emph{be}
    one''. The same arming condition (a run reaching five), the same timing (checked at the next
    event), the opposite direction of obligation \textemdash{} and the receiver's version can be
    \emph{disappointed}, and that is where \code{stuff_error} comes from.
\end{itemize}

The outputs are registered in the same process: \code{data} and \code{real_bit} are levels;
\code{valid}, \code{stuff_error} and \code{real_bit_valid} are the one-cycle pulses
\secref{c15:sec:iface} described.

\section{The process, top to bottom}
\label{c15:sec:process}

One clocked process holds all of the above, plus one concurrent assignment outside it. As in
\chapref{c14:ch}, the walkthrough follows the code in the order it is written. \textbf{The run
tracker} is line for line \chapref{c14:ch}'s, with only the flag renamed after what it now means: the
same procedure, declared inside this module's process and called from every branch taking a bit off
the wire but one, and the exception is noted where it arises. As in \chapref{c14:ch},
\code{collected} is compared against \code{bit_count} via \code{to_integer(unsigned(...))}, so the
file needs \code{use ieee.numeric_std.all;}.

\textbf{The skeleton.} The concurrent line first: \code{data} is an \code{out} port and cannot be
\emph{read}, while a shift is a read-modify-write, so the register is the internal \code{data_reg}
and the port merely mirrors it. Then the process, with its procedure declared before \code{begin},
asynchronous reset first and everything else on the rising edge:

\begin{vhdlcode}
data <= data_reg;  -- Concurrent, outside the process.

process(clock, reset_s2_n) is
    -- One procedure, the run tracker (below).
begin
    if (reset_s2_n = '0') then
        -- Clear everything.
    elsif (rising_edge(clock)) then
        -- Defaults, then the sample branch.
    end if;
end process;
\end{vhdlcode}

\subsection{The run tracker, written once}
\label{c15:sub:tracker}

\Chapref{c14:ch}'s procedure with \code{stuff_expected} in place of \code{stuff_pending}; that single
rename is the whole difference on the receive side. Everything \secref{c14:sub:tracker} says about the
idiom (why it reads the signals directly, why it is declared inside the process rather than in the
architecture, why an \code{in} parameter preserves the pre-edge timing, and why synthesis inlines it)
applies here unchanged:

\begin{vhdlcode}
    -- The run tracker: runs on the bit just taken off the wire.
    procedure track_run(new_bit: in std_logic) is
    begin
        if ((new_bit = last_bit) and (consecutive /= 0)) then
            consecutive <= consecutive + 1;
            if (consecutive = MAX_RUN - 1) then -- Run reaches MAX_RUN.
                stuff_expected <= '1';
            end if;
        else
            consecutive <= 1;                   -- Start a new run.
        end if;
        last_bit <= new_bit;
    end procedure;
\end{vhdlcode}

\subsection{Reset and defaults}
\label{c15:sub:defaults}

\textbf{Reset clears everything}: the group (\code{data_reg}, \code{collected}), the run tracker
(\code{last_bit}, \code{consecutive}, \code{stuff_expected}) and the outputs. This is the
\textbf{only} place the tracker is ever cleared. The groups do not clear it: the transmitter's stream
is continuous over the whole frame, so the receiver's picture of it has to be as well.

\textbf{The defaults next, and only these three}, so that the branches below only \emph{raise} them:

\begin{vhdlcode}
valid          <= '0';
stuff_error    <= '0';
real_bit_valid <= '0';
\end{vhdlcode}

\begin{warning}
\code{stuff_expected} does \textbf{not} belong here, for exactly the same reason
\code{stuff_pending} did not belong in \chapref{c14:ch}'s defaults: it is state, armed at the sample
completing a run and settled at the \emph{next} sample, a whole bit period (fifty clock edges) later.
Default it low and the prediction evaporates before it can fall due, and then stuff bits are neither
discarded nor checked. It is written in exactly three places: cleared at reset, armed inside
\code{track_run}, and cleared when the prediction falls due (both outcomes below).
\end{warning}

\subsection{The sample branch}
\label{c15:sub:sample}

\textbf{\code{(sample = '1') and (enable = '1')} is the only working branch}; there is no
\code{load} on the receive side. It does exactly one of three things, tested in this priority order.
First: an outstanding prediction falls due, and the sampled bit settles it one way or the other:

\begin{vhdlcode}
if ((sample = '1') and (enable = '1')) then
    if (stuff_expected = '1') then
        if (rx_bus /= last_bit) then
            stuff_expected <= '0';    -- The stuff bit arrived; prediction settled.

            track_run(rx_bus);        -- A stuff bit is a bit off the wire like any other.
        else
            stuff_error    <= '1';    -- Six identical bits: a stuffing violation.
            stuff_expected <= '0';
            consecutive    <= 1;      -- Restart the run at the violating bit; the
                                      -- tracker must never count past MAX_RUN.
        end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

The arrived stuff bit is \emph{discarded}: no shift, no change to \code{collected}, and
\code{real_bit_valid} stays \code{'0'}, since a stuff bit is never a real bit (this is the bit
\code{crc15} must not see). The call to the tracker is the mirror image of \chapref{c14:ch}'s in its
stuff branch: a discarded stuff bit resyncs the tracker exactly as a transmitted one does, since the
wire does not distinguish real bits from stuff bits and the tracker must not either. Here it needs no
special argument, since \code{rx_bus /= last_bit} is this branch's own condition, so
\code{track_run(rx_bus)} takes the new-run path by construction and restarts the count at 1.

In the \code{else} branch, six identical bits in a row are impossible on a correctly stuffed bus, so
\code{stuff_error} pulses. The cleanest thing for the rest is to clear the flag, accept nothing, and
restart the run count at the violating bit (\code{last_bit} already holds its value), since it is not
this module's job to decide what a violation \emph{means}. The restart is not optional tidiness:
without it \code{consecutive} stays at \code{MAX_RUN}, the next identical bit runs the tracker's
\code{+ 1}, and the signal's declared range is exceeded; the testbench feeds in a bit past the
violation to pin down exactly that. This is also the one placement \code{track_run} cannot serve, and
for the same reason: \code{rx_bus = last_bit} here, so the call would take the \code{+ 1} path with
\code{consecutive} already at \code{MAX_RUN}. The violating bit is the exception to the rule the
procedure encodes, so its two lines stay written out. \code{can_controller} treats it as fatal and
aborts the reception (\chapref{c17:ch}), exactly as a real controller would.

Otherwise: accept a real bit, shift it in from the right, expose it bit-wise to \code{crc15},
complete the group if this bit completes it, and run the tracker on the bit just accepted:

\begin{vhdlcode}
    else
        data_reg       <= data_reg(6 downto 0) & rx_bus;  -- Shift in from the right.
        real_bit       <= rx_bus;
        real_bit_valid <= '1';

        if (collected = to_integer(unsigned(bit_count)) - 1) then
            valid     <= '1';                 -- This bit completes the chunk.
            collected <= 0;
        else
            collected <= collected + 1;
        end if;

        track_run(rx_bus);   -- rx_bus was just accepted off the wire.
    end if;
end if;
\end{vhdlcode}

The comparison here is \chapref{c14:ch}'s pre-edge signal read: \code{collected} against
\code{bit_count - 1} is the ``run-is-now-full'' idiom applied to the group's completion, the same
shape as \code{consecutive = MAX_RUN - 1} inside \code{track_run}. Nothing else happens at a group
boundary: \code{bit_count} is read live, so the next group simply begins with the next accepted bit at
whatever width \code{can_controller} has set by then, and \code{data_reg} is \emph{not} cleared; the
new group's bits are shifted in beneath whatever the old group left above them
(Exercise~\ref{c15:ex:justify} is about exactly that).

Two mirror notes worth stopping at:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{There is no closing sample.} \Chapref{c14:ch}'s \code{done} had to lag by one shift,
    since a sent bit still needed its full period on the bus. A receiver samples mid-period, once the
    bit has already had its settling time, so the moment the last real bit is accepted the group is
    simply \emph{there}: \code{valid} goes at that very sample, with nothing to wait for.
  \item \textbf{\code{data}/\code{valid} are per group, \code{real_bit}/\code{real_bit_valid} per
    bit.} A whole group is too coarse for \code{crc15}, which needs every real bit as it arrives;
    \code{real_bit} and \code{real_bit_valid} expose exactly the single accepted bit, the receive
    side's counterpart to \code{tx_shift_reg}'s pair \code{bit_valid}/\code{stuff}. Counterpart, not
    mirror image: on the transmit side stuff bits still carry \code{bit_valid = '1'} and the caller
    masks them out with \code{stuff}, while the discarded stuff bit here never raises
    \code{real_bit_valid} at all, so \code{real_bit_valid} alone is already the CRC gating
    (\chapref{c17:ch}).
\end{itemize}

\section{One group, sample by sample}
\label{c15:sec:trace}

Receiving is not simply \chapref{c14:ch} backwards. A transmitter \emph{decides} to insert a stuff
bit; a receiver \emph{predicts} one and then has to check whether it actually came. That difference
is where both of this module's interesting outputs come from.

Here is the wire sequence \code{tx_shift_reg} produced for \code{"11111000"} in
\secref{c14:sec:trace}, arriving at a just-reset \code{rx_shift_reg} with \code{bit_count = 8}. Every
row is a \code{sample} pulse. The \code{run} column is the number of identical bits in a row after the
sample, and \code{data} shows the register filling from below.

\begin{textdrawing}
            rx_bus     action  real_bit  real_bit_valid    run      data  valid
sample 1         1     accept         1               1     1  00000001      0
sample 2         1     accept         1               1     2  00000011      0
sample 3         1     accept         1               1     3  00000111      0
sample 4         1     accept         1               1     4  00001111      0
sample 5         1     accept         1               1     5  00011111      0
sample 6         0    discard         -               0     1  00011111      0
sample 7         0     accept         0               1     2  00111110      0
sample 8         0     accept         0               1     3  01111100      0
sample 9         0     accept         0               1     4  11111000      1
\end{textdrawing}

Nine samples to recover eight bits, exactly the nine presentations \chapref{c14:ch}'s transmitter
needed to send them. Three rows carry the lesson:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sample 5} takes the fifth \code{'1'} in a row. Nothing is discarded here. Reaching
    five only means that the \emph{next} bit is expected to be a stuff bit; this bit is real and is
    collected as usual.
  \item \textbf{Sample 6} is the discarded stuff bit. \code{real_bit_valid} falls to \code{'0'} and
    \code{data} does not move: the group makes no progress. This is the row that matters to
    \chapref{c13:ch}'s engine, since \code{crc15} must not see this bit. The run resyncs to 1 and
    takes the stuff bit's own value as the start of the new run.
  \item \textbf{Sample 9} completes the eighth \emph{real} bit, so \code{valid} pulses and
    \code{data} reads \code{11111000}, the original group. The stuff bit has vanished without
    \code{can_controller} ever finding out it existed.
\end{itemize}

Note that \code{data} fills from the right, one position per accepted bit. After nine samples the
group happens to occupy all eight bits, but a 6-bit group would end up with its bits in
\code{data(5 downto 0)} and leave whatever sits above alone. That is the right-justification, and
Exercise~\ref{c15:ex:justify} is about what sits above it.

\textbf{When the stuff bit does not come.} The same mechanism, one bit different. Six \code{'0'}s in a
row where the sixth should have inverted:

\begin{textdrawing}
            rx_bus    expected     action  real_bit_valid    run  stuff_error
sample 1         0    real bit     accept               1     1            0
sample 2         0    real bit     accept               1     2            0
sample 3         0    real bit     accept               1     3            0
sample 4         0    real bit     accept               1     4            0
sample 5         0    real bit     accept               1     5            0
sample 6         0   stuff '1'  violation               0     1            1
\end{textdrawing}

The violation is detected at sample \textbf{6}, not sample 5. Five identical bits are perfectly legal
and are even required before any stuff bit exists at all; it is the sixth, arriving where an inverted
bit was owed, that is impossible on a correctly stuffed bus. \code{stuff_error} pulses for one cycle
and the module goes on collecting; deciding what a violation \emph{means} belongs to
\code{can_controller}, which aborts the frame (\chapref{c17:ch}).

\section{What the testbench pins down}
\label{c15:sec:tb}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The wire sequence for \code{"11111000"} (stuff bit after the fifth \code{'1'}) is destuffed
    back to \code{"11111000"}, \code{valid} pulses at the last sample, and \code{real_bit_valid} is
    \code{'0'} at the discarded stuff bit and \code{'1'} (with \code{real_bit = rx_bus}) at every
    real bit.
  \item \code{real_bit_valid} is back at \code{'0'} the cycle \emph{after} every sample, so a version
    holding the level fails even if it destuffs perfectly.
  \item Six \code{'1'}s in a row raise \code{stuff_error} at the sixth sample, and a seventh identical
    bit is accepted as usual afterwards: the run count restarted at the violating bit instead of
    counting past \code{MAX_RUN}.
  \item A 6-bit group pulses \code{valid} at its sixth sample, not its eighth, and lands
    right-justified in \code{data(5 downto 0)}.
  \item A 3-bit group fed in immediately afterwards, \textbf{with no reset in between}, picks up the
    new \code{bit_count} and lands in \code{data(2 downto 0)}. This is the group-to-group resizing
    \code{can_controller} performs between every field.
\end{itemize}

\section{Putting it into \code{can_controller}}
\label{c15:sec:wire}

The last of the four CAN blocks, and the first whose port map connects to something other than the
clock and its own signals:

\begin{vhdlcode}
    -- rx_shift_reg.
    signal rxsr_bit_count                     : std_logic_vector(3 downto 0);
    signal rxsr_enable                        : std_logic;
    signal rxsr_data                          : byte_t;
    signal rxsr_valid,    rxsr_stuff_error    : std_logic;
    signal rxsr_real_bit, rxsr_real_bit_valid : std_logic;
\end{vhdlcode}

\begin{vhdlcode}
    rx_shift_reg1: entity work.rx_shift_reg
        port map(clock, reset_s2_n, bt_sample, rx_bus_s2, rxsr_bit_count, rxsr_enable,
                 rxsr_data, rxsr_valid, rxsr_stuff_error, rxsr_real_bit, rxsr_real_bit_valid);
\end{vhdlcode}

Two of the connections there are worth stopping at. \code{sample} comes straight from
\code{bit_timer1}'s output, so the receive path samples on the same 70~\% tick the rest of the design
times against; nothing in between decides when the bus is read. And the bus level is
\code{rx_bus_s2}, the output of \chapref{c12:ch}'s \code{rx_bus_sync} instance, \textbf{not} the
\code{rx_bus} port itself. \code{rx_shift_reg} is the only block that reads the bus, and it reads the
synchronised copy; nothing in the design reads the raw pin. (As with \code{tx_shift_reg}: take the
\code{reset_s2_n} connection as it is for now; \chapref{c17:ch} moves both shift registers to a
per-frame reset.)

With this, \code{can_controller}'s architecture holds all four CAN subblocks and their signals,
alongside \chapref{c12:ch}'s \code{meta_prev}, and no frame behaviour at all. Every remaining
connection between them is \chapref{c16:ch}'s state machine.
