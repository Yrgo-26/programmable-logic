\chapter{Project start: the CAN bus, the frame and \code{can_def}}
\label{c10:ch}

{\large\itshape Why protocols need frames, what CAN actually is, and the project's first VHDL.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Why protocols need frames at all: payload, length field, checksum, and why sender, receiver
    and sequence numbers appear as soon as a link is shared by more than two nodes.
  \item CAN's differential bus and multi-master design.
  \item Dominant and recessive bits, and why arbitration replaces a bus master.
  \item Broadcast plus ID filtering instead of addresses.
  \item The CAN frame field by field: SOF, identifier, RTR, control field, DLC, data, CRC-15, ACK,
    EOF.
  \item Bit stuffing: the five rule, and why it exists.
  \item Wired-AND arbitration, worked through by hand.
  \item Bit timing and the sample point, conceptually.
  \item \code{can_def}: the shared package holding every constant the chapter defines.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This is the first chapter of the group project. Read the project specification in
appendix~\ref{app:project} before you start: it describes the way of working, the modules and what is
assessed. The groups are put together in this session.

\Secref{c10:sec:framing} builds up a small frame format of its own from nothing and motivates every
field CAN then has. It is the book's only C++ material, and it exists to make the rest of the chapter
a comparison rather than an enumeration: CAN answers the same four questions, but answers one of them
entirely differently.

\begin{aside}
\textbf{The protocol reference.} This book is about \emph{building} a CAN node, not about CAN as a
standard. The protocol itself is described in \canbook, a short book written for both halves of a
CAN system \textemdash{} the one building the controller in hardware and the one writing the driver
in software \textemdash{} and it is the protocol reference throughout part~\ref{part:can}.

This chapter gives the working understanding needed to write \code{can_def} and get started; if you
get lost in a field, a rule or a timing relationship, that is where you look it up. For whoever is
building the controller, its \textbf{chapters 1--6 are the requirement specification, bit by bit},
and its chapter~7 describes exactly the simplified controller part~\ref{part:can} builds: what it
implements of the standard and what it deliberately leaves out. Appendix~\ref{app:reading} has the
full chapter map between the two books.
\end{aside}
