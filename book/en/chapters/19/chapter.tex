\chapter{The SPI bridge, \code{can_spi_node} and bring-up}
\label{c19:ch}

{\large\itshape The project's last session, and its capstone: the transaction layer, the top level
tying everything together, and the whole chain verified first in simulation and then on real
hardware.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item System verification: two nodes on a wired-AND bus, and what that testbench does not cover.
  \item The transaction FSM: command byte, four data bytes, latch once on read, commit on the fifth
    byte, and abort when SS goes high early.
  \item \code{can_spi_node}: composing \code{spi_slave}, \code{spi_reg_bridge},
    \code{register_bank} and \code{can_controller} into one node.
  \item Bring-up in two halves: two boards on a shared bus line, then a microcontroller as SPI master
    against them, in eight steps.
  \item What real CAN controllers do that this design does not.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

\code{can_controller} is finished as of \chapref{c17:ch}: transmission, reception and arbitration. It
instantiates \code{bit_timer}, \code{crc15} and both shift registers inside itself, so from outside
the whole controller is a single component with the 15-port register-level interface
\chapref{c11:ch} previewed.

Then two layers go in front of it: \code{register_bank} (\chapref{c18:ch}) turns that interface into
the register map, and \code{spi_reg_bridge} plus the \code{spi_slave} handed out turn the register map
into a five-byte SPI transaction. What the parallel class writes its driver against is the far end of
that chain \textemdash{} the register map and the protocol specification in
appendix~\ref{app:protocol}, never \code{can_controller}'s ports.

\begin{warning}
This is the book's tightest chapter, and it is worth saying outright: two modules get written, the
whole chain has to be simulated, the hardware has to come up, and every group presents.

\textbf{So run ahead.} \code{register_bank} and \code{spi_reg_bridge} do not depend on
\code{can_controller} at all \textemdash{} the former needs only \code{can_def}, the latter only
\code{spi_def} \textemdash{} so both can be written in parallel with the controller, from
\chapref{c10:ch} onwards. A group arriving here with the register bank finished and the transaction
format read has the session for wiring up and debugging. A group starting here will not get through
it all.
\end{warning}
