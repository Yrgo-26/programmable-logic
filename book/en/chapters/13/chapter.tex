\chapter{The CRC-15 engine}
\label{c13:ch}

{\large\itshape One engine, two jobs.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{crc15}: a bit-serial CRC-15 engine.
  \item Why the same engine both generates and checks a CRC, with no mode switch.
  \item Why it nonetheless needs a \code{clear}, and what an aborted frame leaves behind without it.
  \item A short CRC worked through the recursion by hand.
  \item Instantiating \code{crc15} inside \code{can_controller}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

CAN's CRC-15 is computed \textbf{bit-serially}: one bit fed in per enabled clock edge, with no
separate ``compute the checksum now'' step. The property that makes this book's design simple:
\textbf{exactly the same engine, run in exactly the same way, both generates a CRC and checks one.}

\Canref{3} is the protocol side of this chapter: what the polynomial is, which fields are counted in,
and why stuff bits are kept out. This chapter builds the engine; that book says what it is to compute
over.

Design it from the contract and the recursion below, and then verify against the testbench handed
out. \code{crc15} does not depend on \code{bit_timer} and may well have been built already.
