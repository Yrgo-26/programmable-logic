\chapter{Architecture, the top level and the register map}
\label{c11:ch}

{\large\itshape From protocol to block diagram, and the top level every later module is instantiated
in.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Mapping CAN concepts onto hardware blocks: bit timer, CRC engine, shift registers, and the
    frame-sequencing state machine controlling them.
  \item Who owns what: \code{bit_timer} owns \emph{when}, the shift registers own the bit stuffing,
    \code{crc15} knows nothing about frames, and \code{can_controller} is the only block that knows
    the frame format.
  \item A first look at the register map the driver class will consume.
  \item Writing \file{can_controller.vhd} from scratch: its fifteen ports, and an architecture that
    starts empty and grows chapter by chapter.
  \item Which of the ports are asynchronous, and why the controller synchronises them itself.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

\Chapref{c10:ch} gave the protocol. This chapter turns it into an \emph{architecture}: five blocks
with one responsibility each, an entity holding them together, and an interface that stays fixed for
the rest of the book. None of the parts is built here. What is written is the entity they are all to
live in, and its architecture is deliberately left empty.
