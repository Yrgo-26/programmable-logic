\chapter{Timers}
\label{c7:ch}

{\large\itshape A counter with a target value, drawn by hand before it is written.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Timers: a counter raising a flag when it reaches a target value.
  \item Turning clock cycles into real time, and the equality comparator that does it.
  \item The same timer in VHDL, reusable thanks to a \code{generic} rather than through rewiring.
  \item Composing a design out of finished modules instead of rewriting their logic.
  \item \code{walking_led}: a single lit bit walking along a row of LEDs.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

Before the timer runs: predict at which count value \code{timeout} fires, and why that is every
eleventh edge rather than every tenth. That off-by-one is the whole reason the module's generic is
called \code{TICK_COUNT} and not \code{PERIOD}.

\code{walking_led} in \secref{c7:sec:walking} is the payoff from the last four chapters: three
modules, two of them unchanged from \chapref{c4:ch} and one written here, composed into something
you can see from the back row.

\note[Refresher] The timer as a counter with a comparison is in section~6.3 of \debook{}.
