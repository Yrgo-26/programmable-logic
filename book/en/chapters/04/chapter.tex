\chapter{Metastability and synchronisation}
\label{c4:ch}

{\large\itshape Making an asynchronous input safe to use in a synchronous system.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Why an asynchronous input can wreck the clean edge detectors and registers built in
    \chapref{c3:ch}.
  \item Metastability: what it is, and why it cannot simply be designed away.
  \item The two-flip-flop synchroniser, and the ``assert asynchronously, release synchronously''
    pattern for reset.
  \item Reusing the same chain for edge detection, and what it does and does not do about bounce.
  \item Generics: one module serving several sizes.
  \item \code{led_toggle_sync}, driven by a real, bouncing push button.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

Before the synchroniser goes in: predict what a real, asynchronous button does to \chapref{c3:ch}'s
edge detector. That prediction is the reason for the whole chapter.

The argument worth holding on to is why two flip-flops make the failure \emph{unlikely} rather than
impossible. That is the part that does not survive being remembered as a rule of thumb.

\note[Refresher] Metastability, the two-flip-flop synchroniser and what it does not fix are also in
sections~5.7--5.9 of \debook{}.
