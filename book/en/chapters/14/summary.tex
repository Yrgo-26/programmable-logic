\section{Summary}
\label{c14:sec:review}

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\subsection*{What you should be able to do now}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Implement a shift register serialising MSB first and inserting stuff bits per the five rule.
  \item Explain why the stuffing counter must not be cleared when a new group is loaded.
  \item Distinguish \code{bit_valid} from \code{bit_done}, and say which of them the CRC engine
    should be gated on.
  \item Say what \code{stuff} is for, and why it is needed outside the module.
\end{itemize}

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Questions to test yourself with}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Why must the first bit of a newly loaded group be out straight away, instead of at the next
    \code{shift}?
  \item Why is the stuffing counter not cleared on reload?
  \item What would go wrong if \code{can_controller} fed the CRC engine on \code{bit_done} instead of
    on \code{bit_valid and not stuff}?
  \item A stuff bit always has the opposite value to the five preceding ones. Why is ``insert a
    zero'' not enough?
  \item Why does \code{done} wait one extra shift instead of going high straight away when the last
    data bit has been shifted out?
\end{enumerate}

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Target}
About halfway through the project. Three of the four leaf modules are usually finished here, and it
is also about now that it becomes clear which group actually reviews each other's code and which one
just clicks ``Approve''.

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\subsection*{Looking ahead}
\Chapref{c15:ch} builds \code{rx_shift_reg}, the receive side's mirror image: deserialisation,
destuffing and flagging violations of the bit stuffing rule. \Chapref{c16:ch} then puts both
registers to work inside \code{can_controller}.
