\chapter{Transmit shift register and bit stuffing}
\label{c14:ch}

{\large\itshape Serialising out onto the bus, with the same bit stuffing rule as in
\chapref{c10:ch}, now in VHDL.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{tx_shift_reg}: serialisation most significant bit first, and insertion of stuff bits.
  \item Why a loaded group's first bit has to be presented immediately on reload, and why \code{done}
    waits one extra shift.
  \item Why the stuffing state survives across reloads.
  \item \code{stuff} and \code{bit_valid}, and why the CRC engine is gated on them.
  \item Instantiating \code{tx_shift_reg} inside \code{can_controller}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

\code{tx_shift_reg} serialises a group of up to 8 bits MSB first onto the bus and \textbf{inserts} a
stuff bit after five identical transmitted bits in a row. Its mirror image, \code{rx_shift_reg}
(which \emph{discards} those stuff bits), is \chapref{c15:ch}.

The bit stuffing rule itself is unchanged from \secref{c10:sec:stuffing} and \canref{3}, where you
applied it on paper to a whole bit sequence at a time, able to look back over the bits you had
already written down. Here it runs continuously, one bit per clock, with no sequence to look back
over and no ``end of buffer'' to stop at: whatever the rule needs to remember has to live in a
register. \code{can_controller} (\chapref{c16:ch}--\ref{c17:ch}) reuses \textbf{one single}
\code{tx_shift_reg} for every stuffed field, reloading it and changing its bit count group by group.
The unstuffed tail (CRC delimiter, ACK slot and ACK delimiter, EOF) is fixed bits that
\code{can_controller} drives directly, past this register.

This is the first module where drawing the timing diagram before you write the code pays off. Take
the time; most failures here are one-cycle timing errors, not logic errors.
