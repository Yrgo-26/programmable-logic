\chapter{Sequential networks}
\label{c3:ch}

{\large\itshape Storing state: the D latch, the D flip-flop, registers and clocking.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Why combinational logic alone cannot remember anything, and what ``state'' means in a
    circuit.
  \item The D latch and the D flip-flop, and why synchronous designs are built from the flip-flop.
  \item Clocking: period, frequency, and rising versus falling edge.
  \item The synchronous process template in VHDL, and when a signal assignment actually takes
    effect.
  \item Edge detection: turning a level into a pulse one clock cycle long.
  \item \code{led_toggle}: a button press that toggles an LED and keeps it that way.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

Two predictions are worth making before the respective simulation is run, and they are the whole
reason the chapter builds the latch before it builds the flip-flop:
\begin{itemize}
  \item for the latch, what \code{Q} does when \code{enable} goes low while \code{D} is still
    changing.
  \item for the flip-flop, what \code{Q} does when \code{D} changes \emph{between} two clock edges.
\end{itemize}

\note[Refresher] The latch, the flip-flop, the register and edge detection are in sections~5.1--5.6
of \debook{}, if they need brushing up before they are written in VHDL.
