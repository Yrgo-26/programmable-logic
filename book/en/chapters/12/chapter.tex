\chapter{Synchronisation and the bit timer}
\label{c12:ch}

{\large\itshape The first two building blocks going into the empty architecture: the one that knows
nothing at all, and the first that knows something about CAN.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{meta_prev}: the two-flip-flop synchroniser, written once with a width generic, and
    instantiated twice inside \code{can_controller}, for \code{reset_n} and \code{rx_bus}.
  \item \code{bit_timer}: a free-running counter producing the pulses \code{sample} and
    \code{bit_done}.
  \item Running a testbench handed out against each module and reading its report. This is the
    chapter where the GHDL flow gets its third step.
  \item Instantiating both inside \code{can_controller}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

Two modules instead of one, since both are small and they are the design's only blocks holding no
protocol state: \code{meta_prev} knows nothing at all, and \code{bit_timer} knows only \emph{when}.

Design each module from the contract and the behaviour below, and then verify against the testbench
handed out, without reaching for any reference. Appendix~\ref{app:sim} is the project's GHDL
reference.
